# Contributor terms

keep your copyright, and BaryoVM is not released as closed source.

These are the same terms barakoCMS uses for its core. BaryoDev keeps three engines under MPL-2.0
(the barakoCMS core, BaryoVM and the barako CLI) and puts what is built on them under MIT.

There is nothing to sign. Opening a pull request means you agree to what is below.

These terms apply from 29 October 2026. BaryoVM had no contributor terms before that, so earlier
contributions came in under the repository's licence, MPL-2.0, and they stay there.

MPL-2.0 lets anyone, you included, use BaryoVM inside a commercial or closed-source product. That is
the licence working as intended and nothing here changes it.

**1. You give us permission to use your contribution.**
A perpetual, worldwide, royalty-free licence to use, modify, distribute and sublicense what you
contribute, so it can ship as part of BaryoVM and any project that includes it.

**2. MPL-2.0 or MIT. Nothing else.**
Releases containing your contribution are made under MPL-2.0. If BaryoVM ever moves, it may be
released under MIT. Those are the only two licences BaryoDev uses. "An open source licence" would
also cover AGPL, which many companies will not accept, so the two are named instead.

We cannot make a closed-source release of your work. Not "will not": the permission does not
extend that far, so there is nothing to change our minds about later.

**3. You keep your copyright.**
Nothing here transfers ownership. It is a licence, not an assignment. Your contribution stays
yours and you can use it anywhere else you like, including in commercial work of your own.

**4. Patents.**
MPL-2.0 section 2.1(b) is the patent grant, and it applies to your contribution: you license the
patent claims you can license that your contribution, alone or combined with BaryoVM, necessarily
infringes. Section 5.2 ends that grant for anyone who starts patent litigation over BaryoVM. The
licence's own text is pointed at rather than restated, because a second definition is a second one
to keep in step.

**5. You have the right to contribute it.**
Either you wrote it, or you have permission from whoever did. If your employer owns your work,
please check before contributing.

**6. No warranty, and no obligation.**
Your contribution is provided as is. You are not signing up to maintain it, answer questions about
it, or fix it later.

## What is deliberately not here

**No copyright assignment.** Some projects ask you to hand over ownership. This one does not.

**No right to relicense your work as proprietary.** BaryoVM is free at any scale, with no paid
edition, so a permission that would never be used is not held.

**No signing step.** Contributing is the agreement.
